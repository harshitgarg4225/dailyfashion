The idea

Three moments a day.
That’s the whole app.

01

Point the camera at the mirror. One tap. The app names what you’re wearing by itself, on the phone — “wool coat + white tee” — and files the day.

What you get

Not everything is for the world.

Most days are never posted. You still stood at the mirror and made a choice — to feel put together, to feel like yourself, to walk out the door a little taller. The log is where that quiet effort is finally seen: by you. A week of you, a month of you, a year of you.

One photo in the morning Proof you showed up for yourself, kept where only you can see it.
One answer at night You learn which clothes you feel most yourself in — and reach for them on the days that matter.
A price, entered once Cost per wear falls every time you repeat yourself. Rewearing becomes the confident move, not the lazy one.
A set logged at the rack Numbers that remember what you lifted, so every session has something to beat.
One tap on Sunday A week of your actual life as one clean image — for the community, for the story, or for nobody at all.

A good day doesn’t need an audience. It needs a record.

The week

Seven days become one image.

Every Sunday the log builds a card from your week — your days, your best outfit, what was on the hanger. Rendered on your phone, saved by your hand. Post it to Instagram, to the community on Reddit, or to nobody.

Privacy

Your photos have nowhere to go.

Not a promise — a property. The app is only allowed to talk to the host it came from: the browser refuses every other destination before a request leaves your phone, and our server refuses anything shaped like a photo, a note, or how a day felt. Our test suite fails the build if either of those ever stops being true.

Usage counts are shared only if you switch them on, and the switch starts off.
