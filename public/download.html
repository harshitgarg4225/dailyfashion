Daily Fashion

You already know what works.

A private log of what you wore and how the day went. After two weeks it starts telling you what your own days say — with the numbers attached, so you can check every word of it.

  1. One

    A photo in the morning

    One tap. No cataloguing, no tagging, nothing to fill in.

  2. Two

    Two questions at night

    How the day felt, and whether anything happened. Seconds, not minutes.

  3. Three

    Something you couldn’t have noticed

    “Compliments cluster on green. What you actually wear is black.” Every observation arrives with its arithmetic attached.

Get it

Download for Android daily-fashion.apk · Android 7.0 and later Open it in your browser Works on any phone or laptop. Add it to your home screen and it runs offline.

On iPhone, open this site in Safari, tap the share button and choose “Add to Home Screen”. It behaves as an app from then on, camera and all. The App Store version is on its way.

On Android, your phone will ask once whether to allow installs from your browser. That prompt is Android’s, and it appears for anything not installed from the Play Store.

Nothing leaves this phone

No account. No sign-up. No server to hold your photographs, because there is no server at all. Your log lives in your phone’s own storage and nowhere else.

That is a property of the app rather than a promise about it. The page is served under a policy that forbids the browser from making any outbound request whatsoever, and a test fails the build if the app ever attempts one. Turn on airplane mode and everything works exactly the same.

What it will never do

It records clothes and days. There is no field anywhere in it for anything else about you, and there never will be. It has no verdict to offer about you and it never will — every observation it makes is about a garment and a day, drawn from what you yourself wrote down.

It says nothing at all until it has fourteen answered evenings, and it stays quiet about anything it cannot support. An app that guesses early teaches you not to believe it later.
